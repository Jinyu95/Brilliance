3. FMA: strong redistribution, not a certified chaos rate

Frequency analysis used 512 tagged particles and two nonoverlapping 256-cell windows, each equivalent to 14.22 ring turns. Frequencies came from cell-sampled complex normalized coordinates using refined Hann-squared spectral peaks; multiplying by 18 gives full-ring tunes.

At 10 A:

  • Median horizontal tune moves from 4.206 to 4.603.
  • The sampled fraction below (Q_x=4.5) decreases from 88.9% to 18.0%.
  • Median two-window tune-vector change is 0.409, versus 0.0060 at 3 A.

This is consistent with beam expansion reducing tune depression and moving much of the population above resonance. Because the self-consistent field evolves, large FMA changes include systematic tune drift and cannot alone establish chaotic diffusion.

Persistent four-island trapping was not demonstrated: only 3 of 512 particles passed the late-window bounded-angle heuristic in the refined case.

4. Mechanism: the envelope contribution is independently supported

The nonlinear Gaussian space-charge field supplies octupole-like terms, modulated by the FODO periodicity. Their fourth-order resonant harmonic acts near (4Q_x=18), allowing fourfold distortion without magnetic octupoles or magnet ramping.

But the KV control—initially uniform in transverse density—suppresses the early nonlinear distortion and nevertheless develops a large envelope oscillation. The completed one-cell second-moment diagnostic finds [ \lambda\simeq-1.0712\text{ to }-1.0714. ]

The negative multiplier indicates alternating cell-to-cell perturbations. Its magnitude gives an e-folding time of 14.49–14.54 cells, approximately 0.81 turns. The zero-current control is stable within finite-difference accuracy.

Importantly, the saved full-ring KV trajectories independently give 14.46 cells for the first unsaturated growth episode, turns 4–7.8. That agreement supports a genuine envelope instability. It does not determine its quantitative share of the Gaussian emittance growth.

The diagnostic distinction is consistent with Pande’s study of fourth-order resonance and envelope instability. That paper informed the interpretation; the numerical results above are this project’s JuTrack calculations.

Limitations
